Retrofit Radar: Predicting Building Energy Use with MATLAB

Team MatrixNotFound · Learn to Hack: MATLAB Edition 2026
This report walks through our solution using the results saved by main.m. It does not retrain anything, so it runs in seconds. Run main.m first to create results/pipelineResults.mat, results/retrofit_shortlist.csv and the figures in figures/.

1. The problem

Buildings are a major source of energy use and carbon emissions. If we can predict how much energy a building should use from its type, size, age and local climate, then buildings that use far more than that are the first candidates for an energy audit or a retrofit.
We predict site energy use intensity (site_eui, kBtu per square foot per year) for 9,705 buildings from the WiDS Datathon 2022 data, training on 75,757 building-years.
projectRoot = fileparts(which("ProjectReport"));
addpath(fullfile(projectRoot, "src"));
load(fullfile(projectRoot, "results", "pipelineResults.mat"), "finalModel", "cvReport", "yTrain");
figuresDir = fullfile(projectRoot, "figures");

2. What the data told us

Three findings shaped every later decision:
  • The test set is one unseen year. Every test row is Year_Factor 7; training covers years 1 to 6. As a category, year 7 would be meaningless to a model, so we treat the year as a number.
  • The test set has no State_6 buildings, although State_6 is 67% of the training data. The test distribution is shifted.
  • Key fields are missing. energy_star_rating is missing for 35% of training rows, and wind and fog readings are missing for 88-94% of test rows.
Our responses:
  • energy_star_rating is filled with the median of the building's facility type; year_built = 0 is treated as missing.
  • Every fill value is learned from training rows only, and a <name>_missing flag records where data was absent, because missingness itself is informative.
  • We skipped wind-vector features: with 88% of test values missing they would mostly encode filled-in medians.
  • New features with a physical meaning: building age, floor area × heating degree days, floor area × cooling degree days (seasonal heating and cooling load) and Energy Star rating × floor area (the scale of efficiency).

3. Validating the way the test set is built

Random k-fold cross-validation mixes years, and the same building appears in several years, so a model can "recognise" buildings it has already seen. We instead use leave-one-year-out (LOYO) cross-validation: hold out one entire year, train on the other five, repeat for all six years. Imputation and feature engineering are refit inside every training fold, so held-out rows never leak into preprocessing.
The baseline tree shows why this matters: random folds report a noticeably lower error than holding out a year.
disp(cvReport.validationGap)

4. Iterating on the model

We compared four models under LOYO, keeping every attempt in the table:
  1. Regression tree (fitrtree): a single interpretable baseline.
  2. LSBoost v1 (fitrensemble, 300 trees, learning rate 0.1): gradient-boosted trees handle mixed numeric and categorical predictors without scaling and capture non-linear interactions such as size × climate × building type.
  3. LSBoost v2 (shrinkage): learning rate 0.05, 400 trees, minimum leaf of 50 buildings and histogram binning, to reduce over-fitting to year-specific patterns.
  4. LSBoost v3 (feature selection): v1 without the 36 monthly temperature columns. Those values are identical for every building in a state-year, so trees can memorise them as a fingerprint that never matches an unseen year.
The model with the lowest LOYO RMSE is refit on all training data and used for the submission.

5. How accurate is it?

Out-of-fold predictions come from models that never saw the year being predicted, so this plot is an honest picture of accuracy on new data. The model tracks typical buildings well; the largest errors are the rare extreme consumers, which are exactly the buildings worth investigating.
fprintf("Out-of-fold RMSE of the selected model: %.2f\n", ...
computeRMSE(yTrain, cvReport.outOfFoldPredictions));

Facility type and Energy Star rating dominate, and our engineered features (efficiency × floor area, building age, floor area × degree days) are among the strongest predictors.

6. Retrofit Radar: from prediction to action

For each building in the most recent year we compare its actual EUI with the expected EUI from the out-of-fold model, which is what similar buildings in a similar climate use. Multiplying the excess by floor area gives the excess energy in MWh per year, and ranking by it puts the biggest savings opportunities first.
shortlist = readtable(fullfile(projectRoot, "results", "retrofit_shortlist.csv"));

The radar flags buildings for inspection rather than delivering a verdict: a hospital may legitimately run hot, and the anonymised data cannot tell us why a building is high. It does tell an energy manager where to look first.

7. Summary

  • Data: leakage-safe imputation fitted inside every fold, missingness flags and physically meaningful features.
  • Validation: leave-one-year-out cross-validation that mirrors the unseen test year.
  • Model: gradient-boosted trees, improved over a single tree and iterated with shrinkage and feature selection.
  • Impact: a ranked shortlist of the buildings that use the most energy beyond what their peers use.
